## Purpose

Provide the course's main organizing framework: the four phases of the national budget cycle, who decides what in each, which documents appear when, and where citizens can plug in.

## Learning objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Name the four phases of the budget cycle and what happens in each.
2. Identify the key institutions per phase (DBM, DBCC, agencies, House, Senate, bicam, President, COA).
3. Place the major documents (budget call, NEP, GAB, GAA, veto message, execution reports, audit reports) on the cycle.
4. Locate at least one citizen participation point per phase.
5. Explain how each phase feeds the next (including lessons from accountability into the next preparation).

## Lessons

### 04.1 Preparation: Building the Proposal

* Guiding question: Who writes the first draft of the national budget?
* Concepts: fiscal targets and budget priorities (DBCC, macroeconomic assumptions); the budget call; agency proposals; technical budget hearings; executive review; NEP preparation
* Interactive or visual: int-03 timeline (shared across 04.1–04.4)
* Worked example: active-transport budget items (protected bike lanes, service contracting) traced from advocacy input during preparation into the enacted budget — public record of the items, amounts, and dates; the Move As One coalition is named as the documented proponent under the factual-event exception (ADR-008, amended 2026-08), described neutrally with no implied endorsement
* Activity and knowledge check: sequence the preparation steps; 2-item check

### 04.2 Legislation: Congress Decides

* Guiding question: What happens between the NEP and the signed GAA?
* Concepts: NEP submission as the GAB; committee hearings; House and Senate deliberations; bicameral conference; presidential approval and line-item veto; enactment
* Interactive or visual: int-03 timeline continued
* Worked example: none here (participation depth in Module 9)
* Activity and knowledge check: match each step to its chamber/institution; 2-item check

### 04.3 Execution: Money Moves

* Guiding question: What does an agency do with an approved budget?
* Concepts: allotment releases; cash programming and notice of cash allocation; procurement and implementation; agency responsibilities; adjustments and realignments; budget validity periods
* Interactive or visual: int-03 timeline continued
* Worked example: none (full expenditure chain in Module 5)
* Activity and knowledge check: true/false on execution misconceptions; 2-item check

### 04.4 Accountability: Closing the Loop

* Guiding question: Who checks whether the money did what it was supposed to?
* Concepts: financial and physical accomplishment reporting; agency performance review; internal controls and COA audit; congressional and public oversight; Transparency Seal; how findings feed the next cycle
* Interactive or visual: int-03 timeline completed
* Worked example: none here (audit-reading skills in Module 7)
* Activity and knowledge check: match oversight actor to mechanism; 2-item check

## Interactive element

* **int-03 Budget cycle timeline:** the course's core organizing visual. A navigable annual timeline (calendar-shaped, phase-colored) showing per phase: documents produced, decisions made, institutions acting, and citizen participation points. Clicking a month/phase reveals detail panels. Static data; reused as a mini-widget in Module 9 (09.3 entry-point mapping) — where other modules need cycle context, they link to Module 4 rather than embedding the widget. Fallback: static phase-by-phase table.

## Assessment

Module check: 6 items — phase identification, institutions, documents, and participation points.

## Worked examples used

Active-transport budget items (04.1): the strongest neutrality-compliant preparation-phase example — specific line items, amounts, and dates are public record in NEP/GAA volumes, while the engagement story is told generically ("civil society advocacy during preparation"). Amounts (₱33 billion ask; ~₱15.58 billion service-contracting outcome) dated and verified against budget documents at authoring.

## Sourcing notes

Strong guidebook coverage: Chapter 3 and Annex 1 cover all four phases in detail, including budget validity periods and the document set. Verify current-year specifics (dates, validity rules) against DBM's published budget process guides at authoring time.
