## Purpose

Show that an approved budget does not automatically produce results — follow the expenditure chain from appropriation to delivered service, and build a fair, evidence-based view of spending performance.

## Learning objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Trace the expenditure chain: appropriation → allotment → obligation → disbursement → delivery.
2. Explain procurement's place between obligating funds and delivering services.
3. Distinguish physical from financial performance, and outputs from outcomes.
4. List at least three common causes of underspending and implementation delay.
5. Explain why high utilization does not prove good performance, and low utilization does not prove waste.

## Lessons

### 05.1 The Expenditure Chain

* Guiding question: A school building is in the GAA — does that mean a school building exists?
* Concepts: appropriation vs actual spending; the five-stage chain (recap from 03.4, now with mechanics); where each stage is recorded and who controls it
* Interactive or visual: int-04 school-building step-through
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: the step-through is the activity; check asks learner to identify which stage a scenario describes; 2-item check

### 05.2 Procurement: The Long Middle

* Guiding question: Why does it take months between "funds released" and "construction started"?
* Concepts: procurement as the bridge from obligation to delivery; planning, bidding, award, implementation; how procurement timing interacts with budget validity
* Interactive or visual: procurement milestone strip added to the int-04 storyline
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: place procurement milestones in order; 2-item check

### 05.3 Reading Performance Fairly

* Guiding question: An agency spent 95% of its budget — did it perform well?
* Concepts: physical vs financial accomplishment; outputs vs outcomes; utilization ≠ performance in both directions; common causes of underspending (late releases, procurement delays, capacity gaps, right-of-way, design changes) and legitimate reasons for low obligation
* Interactive or visual: two-panel contrast card (high utilization/weak outcomes vs low utilization/legitimate reasons)
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: judgment scenarios — given utilization plus context, choose the fair reading; 3-item check

## Interactive element

* **int-04 School-building step-through:** step-through wizard. One classroom-building project followed across stages: GAA line → allotment → procurement → construction → payment → students enrolled. Each step shows elapsed time, the responsible actor, and the paper trail it leaves (document names from Module 3). Static, illustrative figures with a realistic timeline. Fallback: static storyboard.
