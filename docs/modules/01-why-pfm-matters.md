## Purpose

Connect budgets to public services and everyday outcomes, and establish the four-lens view of the budget (plan, priorities, law, accountability) that the rest of the course builds on.

## Learning objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Define Public Financial Management in one sentence.
2. Explain the budget as a financial plan, a statement of priorities, a legal authorization, and an accountability mechanism.
3. Trace the chain from policy → plan → budget → program → service.
4. Distinguish announcing a program from funding it and implementing it.
5. Name the roles of citizens, officials, oversight institutions, and civil society in PFM.

## Lessons

### 01.1 What Is Public Financial Management?

* Guiding question: When a classroom gets built, who decided that — and where did the money come from?
* Concepts: PFM definition; the budget as the operationalization of collective priorities through government; why every public service has a budget behind it
* Interactive or visual: none (int-01 comes in 01.3)
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: quick matching activity — match public services to the budget decisions behind them; 2-item check

### 01.2 Four Ways to Read a Budget

* Guiding question: Is a budget just a spreadsheet?
* Concepts: budget as financial plan; statement of priorities ("follow the money"); legal authorization (nothing may be spent without appropriation); accountability mechanism (records create the trail audit follows)
* Interactive or visual: four-panel visual card set
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: classify statements about a sample budget line under the correct lens; 3-item check

### 01.3 From Concern to Expenditure

* Guiding question: What happens between "we need a health center" and a health center operating?
* Concepts: policy → plan → budget → program → service chain; announced vs funded vs implemented; where citizen roles plug in
* Interactive or visual: int-01 trace-a-concern click-through
* Worked example: none (case depth comes in Module 9)
* Activity and knowledge check: the trace is the activity; check asks learner to identify the missing link in a broken chain

## Interactive element

* **int-01 Trace-a-concern:** step-through wizard. Learner picks one of three concerns (classrooms, health centers, flood control); each step reveals the next link (need → policy response → budget line → program → delivered service) with a one-line explanation and realistic-but-illustrative figures. Static data. Fallback: static storyboard of the three flows.

## Assessment

Module check: 5 items covering the four lenses and the policy-to-service chain.
