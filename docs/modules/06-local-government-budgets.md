## Purpose

Introduce the distinct PFM structure of local government units — revenue, planning, authorization, execution — and the entry points it opens for citizens.

## Learning objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. List the main sources of local revenue and distinguish them from national sources.
2. Explain the National Tax Allotment and its role in LGU budgets.
3. Describe the local budget preparation and authorization sequence and the roles of the local chief executive and sanggunian.
4. Explain what devolution and the Mandanas-Garcia ruling changed for LGU budgets.
5. Identify the participation bodies (local development council, special bodies) and when citizens can engage.

## Lessons

### 06.1 The Local Purse

* Guiding question: Where does my municipality get the money it spends?
* Concepts: local revenue sources (real property tax, local business taxes, fees and charges, economic enterprises); NTA and other transfers; IRA-to-NTA rename and what it signals
* Interactive or visual: municipal revenue composition chart (static, illustrative)
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: classify local revenue items; 2-item check

### 06.2 The Local Budget Process

* Guiding question: Who signs off on my city's budget?
* Concepts: local development planning and the Local Development Investment Plan; executive preparation; sanggunian authorization; the LCE–sanggunian relationship; review by higher-level bodies (provincial, DILG/DBM as applicable)
* Interactive or visual: local budget calendar strip (mirrors int-03 styling)
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: sequence the local authorization steps; 2-item check

### 06.3 Devolution and Mandanas

* Guiding question: Why did LGU budgets suddenly become a national story?
* Concepts: devolution as the default under the Local Government Code; Mandanas-Garcia ruling and the expanded NTA base; what functions and funds devolved; transition-period challenges
* Interactive or visual: before/after devolution comparison card
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: true/false on devolution misconceptions; 2-item check

### 06.4 Participating Locally

* Guiding question: Where can I plug in before my municipality's budget is set?
* Concepts: local development councils and sectoral committees; special bodies (school board, health board, peace and order council); CSO accreditation; participation timing relative to the local calendar
* Interactive or visual: participation-points map layered on the local budget calendar
* Worked example: an illustrative composite of a city health-emergency response — community listening exercises producing a people's agenda, a coalition seat in the city task force, and measurable outcomes (based on the guidebook's local case, anonymized and labeled as illustrative)
* Activity and knowledge check: match engagement route to body/timing; 2-item check

## Interactive element

* **int-11 Municipal budget builder (Phase 3):** simulation. Learner assembles a simplified municipal budget under revenue, statutory (PS cap, 20% development fund), and sectoral constraints; feedback explains each constraint's rationale. Fallback: static budget worksheet with the same constraints and a worked answer.

## Assessment

Module check: 6 items on revenue, NTA, process roles, devolution, and participation bodies.

## Worked examples used

City health-emergency composite (06.4): adapted from the guidebook's local case (Ch. 1), anonymized per the neutrality rule and labeled as an illustrative composite. It demonstrates local participation end-to-end — listening → people's agenda → task-force seat → measurable results. The method is real and reusable; the named city and organizations stay in the guidebook.

Also note: 06.1's NTA discussion connects back to Module 2's national revenue side (transfers flow from national collections).

## Sourcing notes
