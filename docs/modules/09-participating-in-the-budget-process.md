## Purpose

Translate budget literacy into meaningful engagement: finding the right entry point, preparing evidence, building coalitions, and communicating findings.

## Learning objectives

After this module, the learner can:

1. Explain why citizen participation matters in each phase of the budget cycle.
2. Run a basic community listening exercise and define a problem from it.
3. Identify the correct institution and decision-maker for a given budget concern.
4. Map entry points across the budget cycle (consultations, hearings, oversight mechanisms).
5. Structure an evidence-based budget proposal using Completed Staff Work.
6. Outline how coalitions amplify budget advocacy and how to communicate findings.

## Lessons

### 09.1 Why Participate

* Guiding question: The budget process is technical — does my input actually change anything?
* Concepts: participation as right and responsibility across the cycle; documented instances where civil society engagement moved budget outcomes (₱69 billion and ₱33 billion asks vs enacted figures — attributed generically to civil society coalitions, amounts are public record); what participation realistically shifts
* Interactive or visual: engagement-outcomes timeline strip
* Worked example: the same active-transport items from 04.1, now told as the participation story — asks, engagement, and enacted outcomes
* Activity and knowledge check: match participation claim to phase; 2-item check

### 09.2 Start by Listening

* Guiding question: How do you find out what problem your advocacy should solve?
* Concepts: community listening from the guidebook; problem owners ("skin in the game"); the six-step listening method; going from anger to contribution
* Interactive or visual: six-step listening method card sequence
* Worked example: the city health-emergency composite from 06.4, now focused on the listening method that produced the agenda
* Activity and knowledge check: draft three listening questions for a chosen sector; 2-item check

### 09.3 Find Your Target

* Guiding question: You have an issue — who exactly can act on it?
* Concepts: mapping institutions to issues (national vs local, agency vs legislature vs oversight); identifying the decision-maker and the influencer; timing engagement to the cycle
* Interactive or visual: institution-mapping grid on the int-03 mini-widget
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: route three sample issues to institutions; 3-item check

### 09.4 Build the Proposal

* Guiding question: What does a decision-maker need in order to say yes?
* Concepts: evidence-based proposal components; Completed Staff Work — packaging so the decision-maker only approves or disapproves; the staff-work standard
* Interactive or visual: int-05 engagement plan builder
* Worked example: an illustrative composite of a sectoral budget proposal routed to officials — assembled from the guidebook's proposal patterns (municipal to national scale, pesos-to-programs), labeled as illustrative
* Activity and knowledge check: the plan builder is the activity; check on CSW principles; 2-item check

### 09.5 Advocate with Others

* Guiding question: Why do budget campaigns almost always run as coalitions?
* Concepts: coalition basics from the guidebook (core group, secretariat, shared metrics); lobbying essentials; evidence-based messaging; monitoring implementation and accountability mechanisms
* Interactive or visual: coalition anatomy diagram
* Worked example: none
* Activity and knowledge check: identify coalition roles in a scenario; 2-item check

## Interactive element

* **int-05 Engagement plan builder:** form-to-downloadable-output. Learner picks issue, budget stage (from int-03's cycle), target institution, evidence, message, and desired decision; output is a one-page engagement plan. Entries feed the capstone's familiarity with structured outputs. Static logic. Fallback: worksheet template with the same fields.

## Assessment

Module check: 6 items on entry points, listening method, target-mapping, and CSW.

## Worked examples used

* Active-transport items (09.1): the participation story of the same budget lines used in 04.1 and 07.4 — asks vs enacted outcomes, engagement described generically. Using one thread across three modules gives the course a running example without naming any organization.
* City health-emergency composite (09.2): reprises 06.4 with the listening method as the focus.
* Sectoral-proposal composite (09.4): illustrative, labeled as such; assembled from the guidebook's proposal patterns rather than any named fellow or program.

## Sourcing notes

Strong guidebook coverage: Chapters 1 and 6 plus Annexes 2–3 cover listening, coalitions, CSW, and messaging. Lobbying specifics and communications strategy should follow the guidebook's own cited materials (Making Together Work guidebook) where possible.
